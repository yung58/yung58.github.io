About

I am a third-year Physics Ph.D. student at Georgia Institute of Technology, advised by Audrey Sederberg. My background is in computational neuroscience, vision science, and quantitative biophysics. Previously, I received an M.S. in Biomedical Engineering from Carnegie Mellon University, where I worked with Tai-Sing Lee at the Center for the Neural Basis of Cognition (CNBC).

Research interests

Neural population dynamicsCriticality & scaling lawsLatent structuresconnectivityRecurrent neural networksMachine learning

Latent structure for learning

Connectivity constrains neural dynamics to low-dimensional latent structures that shape learning. I study this link in brains and artificial networks, using structure measured in the brain to design initializations that help artificial networks learn efficiently.

Power-Law Scaling and Brain Function

Power-law scaling describes complex neural dynamics in a simple but informative form. I leverage a statistical latent variable model that links a small number of latent variables to the observed power laws, as a way to understand how brain function emerges.
